Why Your Golden Retriever Sheds Year-Round, and How to Get It Under Control in 2026

The double coat, daylight and health causes behind constant shedding, plus a checker and the groomer method that keeps hair off your floors.

Published: 2026-05-20 · Golden Zone Guide Editors

Why does your golden retriever shed year-round? Because the breed carries a dense double coat that renews itself constantly, and the "twice a year" blow is only the loudest part of that cycle. Light from windows and lamps, age, hormones and diet all shift how much hair comes out in a given week. You cannot switch shedding off, but you can catch most of the loose undercoat in a brush before it lands on your sofa.

Key points in 30 seconds

  • Daylight drives the cycle: moulting in dogs follows melatonin released in response to seasonal sunlight changes, not temperature, according to Wikipedia's summary of mammal moulting research.
  • Two peaks, constant background: most dogs moult twice a year, in spring and autumn. Goldens also drop hair every day in between.
  • Some extra shedding is medical: protein deficiency, hypothyroidism, pregnancy, nursing and recovery from a severe illness can all increase hair loss.
  • "Stopping" it means removing it: professional groomers bathe, rake and force-dry the undercoat out in stages rather than cutting the coat shorter.

Why does a golden retriever shed all year instead of twice a year?

The golden retriever has a double coat. The outer coat is long, flat or wavy, with feathering on the legs. Underneath sits a dense, water-resistant undercoat that insulates the dog in cold and wet weather. Each hair grows, rests and then falls out on its own schedule, and those schedules are not synchronised, so some hair is always dropping. That is the background shedding every golden family knows.

On top of that sits the seasonal moult. Research summarised on Wikipedia's moulting page explains that shedding in dogs, as in all mammals, follows melatonin from the pineal gland. The gland responds to changes in seasonal sunlight rather than temperature. Lengthening days in spring trigger the loss of the thick winter undercoat. Shortening days in autumn bring in a new one. Most dogs moult twice a year this way, and owners call the heavy weeks "blowing coat."

Here is where modern life comes in. A golden that spends most evenings under lamps and TV light gets a blurrier daylight signal than a farm dog. The result is often less dramatic peaks and a steadier trickle all year. So when an indoor golden seems to shed non-stop, the coat is not broken. It is responding to a light cycle that never fully switches seasons.

The video below comes from Go Groomer, a professional pet groomer's channel. It shows a full at-home groom of a golden: bath, de-shedding while the coat is wet, conditioning and combing, then force drying to blow out the loose undercoat.

Which months does a golden retriever shed the most?

Expect two peaks and a lower plateau in between. The exact weeks shift with your latitude, the weather and the individual dog. Use this table as a planning guide, not a promise.

A practical tip: put the start of each peak in your calendar. Raising your brushing frequency a week before the blow starts is easier than catching up once the tumbleweeds appear.

Do age, sex or coat type change how much a golden sheds?

Puppies and the "coat change"

Puppies start with a soft, fluffy coat that is replaced by the adult double coat. One owner in a 75-comment r/goldenretrievers thread from March 2026 said their dog shed heavily until 14 months, when the puppy fur was finally gone. If you are still setting up for a new puppy, our first-week checklist is a good place to add a soft slicker brush and short daily handling sessions, so grooming feels normal before the big coat arrives.

Intact females and heat cycles

Owners often link coat blows to hormones. In the same thread, one owner wrote that intact females blow coat twice a year with their heat cycles, while males tend to blow once a year. That is owner experience, not a study, but it matches what many breeders describe. Pregnancy and nursing are a clearer case, covered in the health section below.

Show lines versus field lines

Coat type varies inside the breed. Goldens bred for conformation shows tend to have longer and finer coats than those bred to work as gundogs. Several owners in the Reddit thread said their dogs with thin undercoats shed far less than a sibling with a thick one. One owner noted that a small red "field" type shed much less than their other two. Choosing a puppy line is one of the few ways to influence shedding before it starts.

When is golden retriever shedding a sign of a health problem?

Most heavy shedding is just the coat cycle. A few patterns are worth a vet visit, though. The Merck Veterinary Manual's guide to hair loss in dogs lists causes that slow or interrupt hair growth:

  • Nutritional deficiencies, particularly a lack of protein.
  • Hormonal imbalances such as hypothyroidism.
  • Pregnancy and lactation, which can cause temporary hair loss.
  • Recovery from a severe illness or fever, with hair loss showing up several weeks later.

The same Merck page notes that excessive shedding can also appear alongside itching, scaling, colour changes or thickened skin. That combination points to a skin condition rather than a seasonal blow.

Why hypothyroidism matters for goldens

Golden retrievers have long been recognised as a high-risk breed for an underactive thyroid. A classic study of 3,206 hypothyroid dogs seen at 15 veterinary teaching hospitals between 1964 and 1978 named golden retrievers first among nine high-risk breeds. It also found that spayed females had a significantly higher risk than intact females. A thyroid problem is not the usual reason a golden sheds, but it is worth ruling out when shedding comes with weight gain, low energy or a dry, thin coat.

Call your vet if you see any of these:

  1. Bald or thin patches, especially the same on both sides of the body.
  2. Red, flaky, smelly or scabby skin, or constant scratching and licking.
  3. Weight gain without eating more, tiredness, or seeking out warm spots.
  4. Coat that stays thin months after a peak instead of growing back.

Can you actually stop a golden retriever from shedding?

No. And owners are blunt about it. When a family asked whether goldens really shed that much, the top replies in the Reddit thread were variations of "a LOT" and "tons." One owner of six goldens over 40 years described them as shedding "year round, all ages." Another said a robot vacuum on every floor once a day keeps it under control. A third said brushing helps, but it does not prevent shedding.

That last point is the key to the title of this article. You cannot stop the coat from releasing hair. You can stop that hair from ending up in your home. Every loose undercoat hair you pull out with a rake or blow out with a dryer is one that will not float under your sofa. The goal is to move shedding from the living room to the grooming spot.

Supplements and "shed-control" foods are often marketed as a cure. A complete diet matters, since Merck links protein deficiency to poor hair growth. But no food will turn a golden into a low-shedding dog. If you change diets, do it gradually and ask your vet first, especially for a dog with sensitive skin.

How do professional groomers get the undercoat out?

Professional groomers treat a golden's coat in layers. A 2021 Groomer to Groomer magazine article on golden retriever grooming describes the core idea. When owners ask for "shorter, shorter, shorter," what they usually want is less hair in the house. So the author advises groomers to think "thinner, thinner, thinner" and remove the undercoat properly instead of trimming the outline.

The workflow in that article runs like this:

  1. Bath and condition. Loose undercoat is raked out while the coat is still conditioned.
  2. High-velocity drying. Forced air lifts the coat off the skin and blows the rest of the undercoat out. The dog is dried all the way through.
  3. Stage one: force dryer and slicker brush. Keep going until this stops pulling out undercoat.
  4. Stage two: undercoat rake. Start right behind the base of the skull and rake through the neck, back and sides. Hold the skin taut above the area you are working.
  5. Stage three: carding knife. Used on areas such as the outside of the rear legs, until the top coat lies smoothly.

The article's verdict is worth remembering. Taking the extra few minutes to card through a double-coated dog is often all it takes to save it from a shave.

What does a weekly de-shedding routine look like at home?

You do not need salon equipment to copy most of this. Here is a 4-week plan our editors suggest for a peak shedding month. Scale it back to two or three short sessions a week outside the peaks.

  1. Week 1: set up. Pick an easy-to-clean spot, ideally outdoors. Get a slicker brush, a wide-toothed undercoat rake and a metal comb. Do short daily sessions of 5 to 10 minutes and pair them with treats.
  2. Week 2: work to the skin. Part the coat with one hand and brush small sections from the skin outward. Spend extra time on the neck ruff, the "pants" on the back legs and the tail, where undercoat packs densely.
  3. Week 3: bath and blow-out. Bathe with a dog shampoo and conditioner, rake while the coat is wet and conditioned, then dry thoroughly. A pet dryer on a cool setting helps a lot. Make sure the undercoat is dry right down to the skin.
  4. Week 4: check and adjust. Run the comb through. If it still snags tufts of soft grey-gold undercoat, go back to the rake. When the comb glides and the top coat lies flat, drop back to every other day.

Two tips make this easier. First, brush outside whenever you can, because fine golden hair floats. Second, keep sessions short and positive. A golden that enjoys grooming will stand still for the 15-minute sessions a peak demands. A golden that dreads it turns every session into a wrestling match. Remember: the brush that matters most is the one that reaches the undercoat. Brushing only the shiny top layer looks tidy for a day and changes almost nothing on your floors.

Will shaving or trimming reduce shedding?

Shaving does not stop a golden from shedding. The hair that grows back is still shed, just in shorter pieces. That is why groomers push the "thinner, not shorter" approach above. We explain the risks in detail in should golden retrievers be shaved.

Light tidying is fine. Trimming the feet, ears and the back of the legs keeps the dog neat and stops mud from collecting. Just do not confuse a tidy outline with less shedding. In summer, a coat with the dead undercoat brushed out is lighter to carry. Pair that with shade, water and sensible walk times. How do you keep the house livable with a shedding golden?

Even with perfect grooming, some hair will escape. Owners in the Reddit thread shared what actually works for them:

  • Daily robot vacuuming on each floor, with an upright vacuum a few times a week for corners and stairs.
  • A vacuum built for long hair. One owner noted that long golden hair clogs weaker vacuums, and others said goldens had worn out several machines.
  • Brushing outside and regular baths followed by a full blow-dry, which owners said "can help a lot."
  • Checking corners and behind doors, where fine golden hair gathers into fluffballs rather than sticking into fabrics.

Add washable covers for the sofa and the car seat, and a lint roller by the front door. Daily walks also count as a grooming chance: a quick brush after you come back keeps loose hair outdoors. Frequently asked questions

Is it normal for my golden retriever to shed every single day?

Yes. Goldens have a double coat in which hairs grow and fall out on their own schedules, so some shedding happens every day. On top of that, most dogs moult twice a year in spring and autumn, when shedding rises sharply.

Why does my indoor golden seem to shed all year with no clear season?

Seasonal moulting is driven by daylight through melatonin, not by temperature. Dogs that spend evenings under artificial light get a weaker seasonal signal, so their peaks are often softer and their shedding steadier through the year.

How often should I brush a golden retriever to control shedding?

A few times a week is usually enough outside the peaks. During a spring or autumn blow, short daily sessions of 10 to 15 minutes that reach the undercoat work better than one long session at the weekend.

Can food or supplements stop a golden retriever from shedding?

No food stops shedding. A complete diet does matter, because the Merck Veterinary Manual lists protein deficiency among the causes of poor hair growth. Talk to your vet before switching food or adding supplements.

When should I worry about my golden's shedding?

See your vet if you notice bald or thin patches, red or itchy skin, or shedding together with weight gain and tiredness. Golden retrievers are a high-risk breed for hypothyroidism, which can cause excessive shedding and a thin, dry coat.

Do female golden retrievers shed more than males?

Owners often report that intact females blow coat with their heat cycles, while males blow once a year. Pregnancy and nursing can also cause temporary hair loss. Individual coat type varies more than sex does.

What is the fastest way to remove a blown golden coat?

Bathe and condition, rake out loose undercoat while the coat is wet, then dry the dog thoroughly with forced air. Groomers follow that with a slicker brush, an undercoat rake and, if needed, a carding knife until the top coat lies flat.

The bottom line: your next 30 days

Your golden retriever sheds year-round because of a double coat that renews itself all the time, plus two daylight-driven peaks. Indoor light, age and hormones shift the pattern. You cannot stop the shedding, but you can decide where the hair ends up. This week, buy or check a slicker brush and an undercoat rake, and mark the next peak in your calendar. Over the next two weeks, build short daily sessions that reach the skin. By the end of the month, add a bath and full blow-dry. And if the shedding comes with bald patches, itchy skin or a change in energy, let your vet take a look.
